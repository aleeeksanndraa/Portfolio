tictaps

Skill-based mobile gaming app · UI/UX redesign · Design system

TicTaps is a skill-based mobile gaming startup preparing for launch. The app lets users play mini-games with friends, create or join parties, take part in tournaments, and compete using in-game currency or real-money gameplay.

I joined the project at an early production stage, when the main screens already existed in Figma but had not yet been fully developed. The team needed a cleaner visual direction, a stronger design system and production-ready components for both iOS and Android.

02

overview

I joined the project at an early production stage.

TicTaps already had an initial design direction, but the interface needed to feel more modern, consistent and easier to build. When the main screens existed in Figma but had not yet been fully developed, the team needed a cleaner visual direction, a stronger design system and production-ready components for both iOS and Android.

The goal was to redesign the mobile experience, improve the visual system and prepare the product for development while keeping it playful, bold and energetic.

04

challenge

Balancing bright gaming visuals with a structured design system.

The main challenge was making the interface feel energetic and playful while keeping it consistent and scalable. The previous UI was inconsistent and difficult to scale, which made it harder to extend the product and support development.

I helped rebuild the system with clearer components, tokens and reusable patterns — creating a foundation that could grow with the product.

06

my role

I redesigned existing screens, created new flows and built production-ready components that the development team could hand off directly. The work spanned the full product surface: games homepage, wallet, friend requests, party creation, profile, leaderboard and gameplay-related screens.

Together with the product manager, we refined the main user flows and made sure every screen connected logically to the next. The aim was to make the app feel easy to navigate and coherent across all states.

08

design system

I rebuilt the UI kit into a more scalable design system with reusable buttons, cards, navigation, modals, inputs and key mobile components. The visual direction is playful, colorful, social and futuristic — bright blue and purple gradients with neon accents.

09

responsive / platform

The visual direction needed to feel gaming-first — energetic, bold and social — while staying functional and easy to build from. Bright gradients, neon accents and strong typographic hierarchy gave the product personality without making it chaotic.

Components were built to adapt to both iOS and Android conventions: navigation patterns, touch targets and type scales were considered for each platform so the system could extend without forking.

10

launch assets / extras

Alongside the app redesign, I created the full App Store and Google Play asset suite — screenshots, feature graphics and promotional banners — formatted to each store's specifications. I also designed a responsive landing page in Webflow to support the product launch.

The redesign includes around 50 mobile screens, updated user flows, a refreshed UI kit, production-ready components and App Store / Google Play assets. The app is currently preparing for launch, with development in progress.

12

reflection

What I took away from this project.

This project helped me understand how design decisions move from Figma into real product development. Working closely with developers made me much more intentional about how I named components, structured variants and wrote annotations — small decisions that save a lot of back-and-forth during handoff.

It also reinforced how important visual consistency is at scale. When a product spans 50+ screens across two platforms, small inconsistencies compound quickly. Building the design system properly from the start made everything downstream easier.
